## Two kinds of bad

Arista's guides call a link that has failed completely, and cannot pass traffic, an **outage**. A link that is still up but cannot deliver the quality an application needs, in loss, latency or
jitter, is a **brownout**. The distinction is per application: a link may be fine for a file transfer and in a brownout for a voice call.

<Callout variant="info" title="A word you will also hear">
  <Term id="blackout" /> is older and common industry wording for a completely failed link. The legacy 2017 white paper uses it. Arista's current guides say outage, and so does this course.
</Callout>

## Why brownouts are harder

An outage is a clear event: nothing arrives. A brownout is a gradual one: a link that drops one packet in a few dozen looks alive to an interface counter. DMPO's per-path measurements of loss, latency and jitter are what make a brownout visible as something other than "up".

## What DMPO does

DMPO detects an SLA failure or an outage within **300 to 500 ms**. What it then does depends on the situation:

| Situation | Typical reaction |
|---|---|
| Outage, another healthy link exists, strictness allows moving | steers the flow to the healthy link |
| Brownout, a healthy link exists | steers the flow away (Preferred) or applies remediation (Available, Mandatory) |
| Brownout on every link, or only one link | on-demand remediation, which the next lesson covers |
| Outage under Mandatory | no steering; packets are dropped by policy |

## The one-minute hold

When a brownout or outage clears, DMPO waits **one minute** before steering back to a preferred link named in the policy. This stops a link that flaps from dragging a call back and forth.

<Callout variant="gotcha" title="A recovered link may stay quiet for a minute">
  If you restore a circuit and the traffic does not come back at once, that can be the hold, not a fault.
</Callout>

## What you would see in the VCO

- **Monitor > Events** records link events, for example one raised when no packets arrive on a link for seven seconds.
- **Monitor > Edges > Links** shows each link's state and measurements, so you can tell a down link from a degraded one.

## Key takeaways

- Outage: fails completely. Brownout: up but below the application's SLA.
- DMPO acts within 300 to 500 ms.
- Strictness decides whether DMPO moves, remediates or drops.
- One-minute hold before moving back.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- Do the events show a link going down, or only measurements getting worse?
- Is the link now carrying traffic again, and is it within the first minute?
