**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- describe the jobs of the <Term id="vco">Orchestrator</Term> and the portals people use to reach it;
- say what Edges and Gateways send to it and how often, and how it notices that one has gone quiet;
- explain configuration in layers (segments and network services feed Profiles, Profiles feed Edges);
- list what the documentation says happens, and what it does not say, when the Orchestrator cannot be reached.

## One portal, several jobs

In Arista's Orchestrator guide the Orchestrator "powers the management plane". That is the course's word for it too: it is where you
**configure**, **monitor** and **troubleshoot**. It is multi-tenant, and it can be hosted, dedicated or run on-premises. Different people
see different views of it: operators have an Operator portal (where, for example, Gateways are managed and system properties are set),
partners have a Partner portal, and enterprises use the SD-WAN service of the Enterprise portal, which is the part this course mostly
shows.

## What runs inside, just enough to understand the traffic

The guide names three Orchestrator processes, and the second explains much of what you will later see in firewall logs and monitoring:

| Process | Job |
|---|---|
| **Portal** | Handles the user interface and API requests from people and scripts |
| **Upload** | Handles incoming requests from Edges and Gateways: activations, heartbeats, flow statistics, link statistics and routing information |
| **Backend** | A job runner for scheduled cleanup and rollups and for queued statistics processing |

New statistics reach the Orchestrator every five minutes, which is why Arista advises against polling the API more often than every ten
minutes for statistics.

## Configuration in layers

You met <Term id="profile">Profiles</Term> in Section 1. The picture to hold now is three layers:

1. **Building blocks**: <Term id="segment">segments</Term> (configured under **Configure > Segments**) and network services (under **Configure > Network Services**);
2. **Profiles**, which combine configuration from segments and network services and are applied to many Edges;
3. **Edges**, which inherit from their Profile and can <Term id="override">override</Term> individual settings.

The Orchestrator also holds routing policy for enterprises. Arista says it manages routes for all enterprises and can override default
routing behaviour. The details of each layer come in the Configuration section; here we only need to know that the Orchestrator is where
the layers live.

## Monitoring and telemetry

Edges and Gateways report to the Orchestrator, which keeps the history. The guide lists default retention periods: a year for link
statistics, <Term id="link-quality">link quality</Term> and Edge health statistics and for events; two weeks for path statistics and for high-resolution flow
statistics; and a year, at one-hour rollup, for low-resolution flow statistics. Link Insights, a monitoring feature added to the
on-premises Orchestrator in 7.0, has the Orchestrator measure link degradation at 30-second intervals.

The Orchestrator also decides when a device is "gone". If it sees no <Term id="heartbeat" /> from an Edge for a configured time, it shows
the Edge as OFFLINE, and if it receives no link statistics for a link for another configured time, it shows the link as unstable and
then disconnected.

## Trust and software

- **Certificates.** The Orchestrator has a built-in certificate authority that issues X.509 certificates to Edges and Gateways. They are used to
  authenticate the management-plane TLS tunnels between the Orchestrator and its devices and the IKEv2/<Term id="ipsec">IPsec</Term> tunnels between Edges and
  Gateways.
- **Software.** Edge software images are managed from the Orchestrator, with per-Edge overrides, and enterprise users can upgrade one Edge,
  a set, or all of them.

## If the Orchestrator is unreachable

This is the question people ask first, and the honest answer is "some of it is documented". Here is what we can say, and what we cannot.

| Situation | What the documentation says |
|---|---|
| A Gateway loses its Orchestrator connection and does **not** reboot | All of its control-plane modules keep working normally |
| A Gateway **restarts** while disconnected | Features that need the control-plane configuration (Non SD-WAN Destinations, VLAN/VRF handoff, BGP) do not work until the connection returns, because that configuration is never cached locally |
| The Orchestrator itself fails over (disaster recovery) | It runs as an active/standby pair with a manual failover; Edges and Gateways cache the monitoring data collected during the outage until the standby is promoted |
| Route preferences while an Edge or Gateway is out of contact | A mechanism called Distributed Cost Calculation (<Term id="dcc" />) moves route-preference calculation to the Edges and Gateways |
| An Edge's traffic forwarding while it cannot reach the Orchestrator | **Not stated** in the pages we read |

<Callout variant="warning" title="Do not assume the missing row">
  It is tempting to write "the network keeps forwarding without the Orchestrator". It may well be true, but the documents we read do not
  say it, so this course does not. Look up your own release's documentation on Edge behaviour during Orchestrator loss before you rely on it
  in a design.
</Callout>

## Key takeaways

- The Orchestrator is the management plane: configure, monitor, troubleshoot, and issue certificates.
- Edges and Gateways report to its Upload process: activations, heartbeats, statistics, routing information.
- Configuration is layered: building blocks, then Profiles, then Edges with overrides.
- What happens to Edge forwarding without the Orchestrator is not documented in our sources, so we do not claim it.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- **Monitor > Edges**: the status column and the per-Edge tabs show what the Orchestrator knows about each Edge. An Edge that has stopped sending heartbeats is marked OFFLINE by the Orchestrator.
- **Configure > Segments**, **Configure > Network Services** and **Configure > Profiles**: the layers described above.
- **Service Settings > Edge Management**: where Edge software images are managed.
- For an on-premises Orchestrator, a disaster-recovery view called **Replication** is reached through the Orchestrator tab. (We read this
  path in the on-premises guide; the hosted service may differ.)
