**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- list the flows an Edge needs through a firewall, with port, protocol and direction;
- explain what <Term id="nat" /> and <Term id="nat-traversal" /> mean for those flows;
- list what a Partner Gateway or an on-premises Orchestrator needs allowed;
- tell the confirmed rules from the ones you must check in your own release's documentation.

## Why a table

Firewall requests are written as rows: from where, to where, port, direction. So this lesson is a table. **Only rows marked confirmed are definite
statements**; the others are flagged so that you know to check your release documentation.

<Diagram id="arch-firewall-flows" />

## The flows of an Edge

| Flow | Port / protocol | Direction | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edge to the Orchestrator | **TCP 443** | outbound from the Edge | Confirmed (the virtual-Edge guide) |
| Edge to a Gateway (tunnel, <Term id="vcmp" />) | **UDP 2426** | outbound from the Edge | Confirmed |
| Edge to another Edge (direct tunnel) | **UDP 2426** | outbound; incoming 2426 must also reach the other Edge, even behind NAT | Confirmed |
| Edge as a **Hub**, from the branches | UDP 2426 | inbound to the Hub | **Check your release docs** |
| DNS and time | UDP 53, UDP 123 | outbound | **Check your release docs** |

<Callout variant="warning" title="Two rows we could not confirm">
  The inbound rule for an Edge that acts as a Hub, and the DNS and NTP rules, appear in a Broadcom knowledge-base article that our tools could not open, so we
  have only seen them in search snippets. They are very likely needed in real deployments, but this course will not state them as confirmed. Check
  the firewall section of the documentation for your release before you finalize a rule set.
</Callout>

## NAT and the tunnel ports

Arista's guide says VeloCloud forces NAT traversal on, using the special port 2426, so the behaviour of VeloCloud is the same whether or not
NAT is present. It also lists UDP 500 and UDP 4500 for IKEv2 negotiation and IPsec NAT transparency between tunnel endpoints, and IP protocol
50 (ESP) for IPsec when no NAT exists between them.

<Callout variant="gotcha" title="Do not build a rule set from this paragraph alone">
  How the ESP entry and the always-on NAT traversal port fit together in each case is not spelled out in the pages we read. The practical rules
  are the ones in the table. If you are asked to open UDP 500, UDP 4500 or ESP, treat it as a question for your release documentation.
</Callout>

## Other components

A Gateway and an Orchestrator can sit behind firewalls too. What the guides say:

| Component | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Gateway to the Orchestrator | outbound TCP 443 | Confirmed (Partner Guide) |
| Partner Gateway, from the Internet | inbound **UDP 2426**, **UDP 4500**, **UDP 500**; plus **IP protocol 50 (ESP)** when NAT is not used | Confirmed |
| Partner Gateway behind NAT | translate those ports to an externally reachable IP address; 1:1 NAT and port translation are both supported | Confirmed |
| On-premises Orchestrator, upstream firewall | inbound **HTTP (TCP 80)** and **HTTPS (TCP 443)**; plus established outbound connections for upgrades and security updates | Confirmed (Orchestrator guide) |
| Browsers to a hosted Orchestrator | HTTPS | Not covered by the pages we read |

Every management flow we found in the guides is HTTPS (TCP 443), including the Orchestrator's own web interface; the persistent connection
between the Orchestrator and an Edge is a WebSocket over TCP (the guides do not give its port). The guides do not say that 443 is the *only*
management port.

## A planning checklist

1. Allow **outbound TCP 443** from Edges and Gateways to the Orchestrator.
2. Allow **outbound UDP 2426** from Edges (to Gateways, Hubs and other Edges).
3. If you want direct branch-to-branch tunnels, make sure NAT devices in front of Edges allow **incoming UDP 2426** from other Edges (or accept the Gateway or Hub path).
4. For Hubs, Partner Gateways and on-premises components, use the inbound rules above, and **confirm** the unconfirmed rows in your release's documentation.
5. Test with the tools in the Orchestrator rather than by guessing (below).

## Key takeaways

- Edge: outbound TCP 443 to the Orchestrator, outbound UDP 2426 to Gateways and Edges.
- Behind NAT, direct tunnels also need incoming UDP 2426 from other Edges, or they will not likely come up.
- Partner Gateway: inbound UDP 2426, 4500, 500, and ESP when NAT is not used. On-prem Orchestrator: inbound 80 and 443.
- Hub inbound rules and DNS/NTP are not confirmed in our sources: check your release docs.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- **Monitor > Edges**: an Edge that cannot reach the Orchestrator stops sending heartbeats and is marked OFFLINE once the configured limit passes.
- **Remote Diagnostics > List Paths**: shows which tunnels are up and the Gateway status for an Edge you can reach.
- **Remote Diagnostics > VPN Test**: shows branch-to-branch connectivity.
- Edge settings: the **UDP hole punching** option on an overlay interface, for NAT cases where incoming tunnels are blocked.
