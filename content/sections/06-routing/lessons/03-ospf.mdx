**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- say where OSPF is enabled and on which interfaces it fully works;
- explain what the Edge learns from OSPF and what reaches the overlay;
- explain the metric types E1 and E2 and the documented defaults;
- say what route summarization is and what the VCO's summarization table covers.

## Enabling it

<Term id="ospf">OSPF</Term> is switched on in two places, in this order:

1. **Profile:** **Configure > Profiles > (Profile) > Device > Routing & NAT > OSPF**. Here you define the areas. Each area has an Area ID; **Normal** is the default and the only supported <Term id="ospf-area">area</Term> type. The dynamic routing page says these settings cannot be overridden at the Edge level, although the Edge device-settings page lists OSPF among settings that offer Override (Section 4); the two pages do not agree, so check your release.
2. **Edge interface:** then enable OSPF on the Edge's interfaces.

Configure OSPF only on the **Global Segment**. OSPFv2 is IPv4; OSPFv3 is IPv6.

## Routed interfaces versus VLAN interfaces

The guide says OSPF can be enabled on a LAN (routed or switched) or WAN interface of an Edge, but "to get full OSPF functionality" you must use a **routed** interface. On a **switched** (VLAN) interface the Edge only supports OSPF as a **passive** interface: the guides we read do not say what passive does there. In general OSPF a passive interface sends no hellos and forms no neighbors, and the simulator assumes that.

## What the Edge learns and advertises

<Diagram id="rt-lan-to-lan" />

- **Learned from the LAN router:** routes arrive as intra-area (O), inter-area (O IA) or external (O E1, O E2). The Edge keeps the lowest cost among routes of the same type.
- **Into the overlay:** routes learned through OSPF are automatically redistributed to the Gateway. But <Term id="overlay-flow-control">Overlay Flow Control</Term>'s **<Term id="global-advertise-flags">Global Advertise Flags</Term>** keep **External OSPF routes** out of the overlay by default, so E1 and E2 routes need their flag turned on (lesson 7).
- **Out to the LAN router:** what the Edge injects into OSPF is set by the Profile's route settings: the **Overlay Prefixes** check box, the **BGP** option for BGP routes, and a default route with route type O1 or O2 (None by default; Advertise Conditional means "only if the Edge learns it").

## E1 versus E2

Routes redistributed *into* OSPF become external LSAs (<Term id="ospf-e1-e2">E1 or E2</Term>). In the Profile you set the **metric** (default 20) and the **metric type**, E1 or E2; **the default is E2**. When OSPF-learned routes compete, the type order is intra-area, then inter-area, then E1, then E2.

## Summarization

<Term id="route-summarization">Summarization</Term> replaces several prefixes with one covering prefix.

<Diagram id="rt-summary" />

The OSPF page has a **Route Summarization** table with Subnet, No Advertise, Tag, Metric Type and Metric. It acts on **external (Type-5)** routes under a supernet: with **No Advertise**, those external routes are summarized and the supernet is not advertised to the peer. Summarizing OSPF-learned routes *before* they enter the overlay is not described in the pages we read.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Do not assume">
  The default state of Overlay Prefixes, OSPF cost and timer defaults, route and neighbor limits, and whether inbound filters accept by default were not stated. Check them in your VCO before building on them.
</Callout>

## What you would see in the VCO

- **Configure > Profiles > (Profile) > Device > Routing & NAT > OSPF** for areas, <Term id="redistribution">redistribution</Term>, route filters and summarization.
- **Diagnostics:** **Show OSPF Neighbors** (expect Full; Down, Attempt or Init for long: check Area ID, Neighbor ID and Router ID; stuck in 2-way or ExStart: check MTU), **List OSPF Routes**, **List OSPF Redistributed Routes**, **Show OSPF Setting**.

## Key takeaways

- Enable at the Profile first, then on Edge interfaces; Global Segment only.
- Routed interfaces give full OSPF; switched ones are passive only.
- Redistributed routes default to metric 20, type E2; type order is O, O IA, O E1, O E2.
- External OSPF routes are kept out of the overlay by default.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- Is the neighbor in Full state?
- Is the prefix in List OSPF Routes, and of which type?
- Is the External OSPF flag on, if the prefix is E1 or E2?
