## What it is

Overlay Flow Control (OFC) is a page in the Enterprise portal: **Configure > Overlay Flow Control**. It shows a summary of the routes in the company's network and the order of exits for them. Its configuration determines the *initial preference values* of routes. It has three parts.

- **Preferred VPN Exits**: the priority of the destinations to which traffic for a route is sent.
- **Global Advertise Flags**: which kinds of routes (static, connected, internal, external and uplink) the system adds to the overlay.
- **Routes List**: columns Segment, Subnet, Preferred VPN Exits, Route Type, Last Update, Created On, IPv4 Subnet and Eligible VPN Exits.

## Global Advertise Flags

<Diagram id="rt-ofc-exit" />

By default the system restricts certain route types from entering the overlay: **External OSPF routes** and **NSD iBGP routes** stay out until you enable their flags. A new flag applies to any route learned afterwards. A hybrid Hub and Branch Edge ignores the Hub Profile's flags and follows the Branch Profile's flags.

## Preferred VPN Exits and pinning

For a particular subnet you can reorder the Preferred VPN Exits with **Edit**. The Orchestrator then sends the change to the relevant Edges, which override their previous preferences. You can also **pin** a route: select it in the Routes List and choose Pin Learned Route Preference; pinned routes get preference values starting from 1, the highest priority. **Refresh Routes** makes Edges and Gateways recalculate costs when distributed cost calculation is on, and briefly affects traffic at all sites, so use a maintenance window.

## How it relates to the rest

- **Route preference:** OFC (or distributed cost calculation) provides the ordering for the *dynamic* routes at step 8 of the order of operations. It does not override longest prefix, connected or local static routes.
- **Hubs (Section 5):** when an Edge learns a route through a Hub, its transit metric is 32 plus the Hub's order value; through a Gateway it is 90; from a directly connected peer Edge, 0. Hub order breaks ties after routing attributes.
- **When to use it:** when the default order of exits is not the one you want for a subnet, or a route type is being held out of the overlay.

## What you would see in the VCO

- **Configure > Overlay Flow Control**: Preferred VPN Exits, Global Advertise Flags, Routes List, Refresh Routes.
- **Diagnostics > List Routes per Prefix** on an Edge: the order of route entries.
- **Device > Routing & NAT > Overlay Route Control** for community and AS-path action on prefixes advertised to the overlay.

## Key takeaways

- OFC sets initial route preferences and which route kinds enter the overlay.
- External OSPF and NSD iBGP stay out by default.
- Editing exits is pushed to the relevant Edges; pinning gives the highest priority.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- Is the prefix in the Routes List, and what are its exits?
- Is the flag for its route kind on?
- Has someone pinned it?
