## Filters, not route maps

Many routers use a *route map*: a list of match-and-set statements. The VCO's equivalent for BGP is a **Filter List** under the BGP settings. You build a filter, then attach it to a neighbor as its **Inbound Filter** (what you accept) or **Outbound Filter** (what you advertise). The word "route map" does not appear in the pages we read. OSPF has simpler **route filters**: inbound controls what OSPF-learned routes are installed into Overlay Flow Control, outbound controls what is redistributed into OSPF.

A BGP filter rule has:

| part | options |
|---|---|
| Match type | Prefix (IPv4 or IPv6), Community, AS Path (regular expression), Metric (the MED value), Local preference |
| Exact Match | on by default |
| Action type | Permit or Deny |
| Action set | None, Local Preference, Community (with Additive), Metric, As-Path-Prepend, As-Path-Exclude |

The limit is 512 BGPv4 match/set rules (256 inbound, 256 outbound). The 6.4 text we read mentions As-Path-Prepend only in the NSD and Gateway sections, and the enhanced options only apply from Edge 7.0, so check your release.

## The attributes

<Diagram id="rt-bgp-attributes" />

- <Term id="local-preference" />: the diagnostics say that between two BGP routes the one with the **higher** local preference wins.
- **AS-path length**: the route with the **shorter** AS path wins; <Term id="as-path-prepend" /> makes your path look longer, so it makes a route less attractive. As-Path-Exclude removes listed AS numbers.
- **Metric (MED)**: the **lower** metric wins.
- <Term id="community" />: a tag you attach or match on; **Uplink Community** marks routes that arrive over an uplink.

The diagnostics name all three comparisons but do not give the order in which the Edge applies them; standard BGP applies local preference, then AS-path length, then MED, and we treat that as a general assumption. For iBGP to a Non SD-WAN site, local preference cannot be set by an outbound filter; use Metric or AS-path prepend.

## Worked example: make site A the preferred exit

Branches B and C reach a data center prefix 10.30.0.0/24 (illustrative) that sites A and D both connect to. You want A to be the exit.

1. **Inside one Edge**, if two BGP neighbors offer the prefix, add an inbound filter on the neighbor towards A: Match Prefix, Action Permit, Action set Local Preference with a higher number. That makes this Edge prefer A's neighbor.
2. **Between Edges**, the guides say you can steer the outcome with **<Term id="route-pinning">route pinning</Term>**, BGP local preference or OSPF costs, and the OFC **<Term id="preferred-vpn-exits">Preferred VPN Exits</Term>** list lets you reorder exits for a particular subnet (lesson 7). Reordering is the most direct documented control.
3. **Verify** with **Show BGP Routes per Prefix** (the best path and the attributes compared) and **List Routes per Prefix** on a remote Edge: the first entry is the best path.

<Callout variant="warning" title="What we cannot promise">
  The pages do not state how a local preference set at A is carried to B and C across the overlay, nor the order the Edge applies attributes in. Use step 2 and step 3 to confirm in your VCO instead of assuming step 1 is enough.
</Callout>

## What you would see in the VCO

- **Configure > Edges or Profiles > Device > Routing & NAT > BGP > Filter List**, then the neighbor's Inbound and Outbound filter drop-downs.
- **Show BGP Neighbor Details** lists attached filters and the prefixes accepted after the inbound filter.
- **Configure > Overlay Flow Control > Preferred VPN Exits**.

## Key takeaways

- A filter is match plus action, attached inbound or outbound on a neighbor.
- Higher local preference, shorter AS path and lower metric win.
- Prepend makes a route worse; there is no route-map object in the VCO.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- Which filter is on this neighbor, inbound and outbound?
- What did Show BGP Routes per Prefix say won, and on which attribute?
- Did you check the remote Edge, not just the one you changed?
