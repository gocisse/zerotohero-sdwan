## The three components

VeloCloud SD-WAN is built from three kinds of component. This course abbreviates them **VCE**, **VCO** and **VCG** (the next lesson
explains the names), but Arista's documents mostly just write Edge, Orchestrator and Gateway.

| Component | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Edge** (<Term id="vce" />) | Hardware appliance or virtual software at a site | Connects local users to the WAN, uses the site's circuits, builds tunnels, applies policy |
| **Orchestrator** (<Term id="vco" />) | Multi-tenant, cloud-based configuration and monitoring portal | Where you configure Edges, write policy and watch the network; Edges get their configuration from it |
| **Gateway** (<Term id="vcg" />) | Cloud-delivered service shared by many customers, deployed in many regions | Gives Edges a nearby on-ramp to cloud services; passes routes between Edges so they do not need a full mesh |

Arista describes the Gateway as autonomous, stateless and horizontally scalable, deployed geographically for low latency. Its brief adds
that Gateways implement the same DMPO (covered below) and give cloud services (SaaS, IaaS) a way to reach each Edge.

## Underlay and overlay

The circuits a site is plugged into (an Internet line, an MPLS circuit, LTE) are what we call the <Term id="underlay" />. VeloCloud
builds an <Term id="overlay" /> on top of them: a set of **tunnels** between Edges and Gateways, and directly between Edges. User
traffic travels inside those tunnels.

Which tunnels exist? From Arista's Administration Guide:

- every Edge creates tunnels to one or more **Gateways**;
- Edges can also create **direct tunnels to each other**;
- with **Branch to Hub VPN** enabled, the Orchestrator tells all Edges to build permanent tunnels to the **Hub** Edges you choose; a Hub
  is an Edge you designate in the Profile, typically placed in a data center;
- with **Branch to Branch VPN** the traffic between branches goes via a Gateway or via a Hub, and with **Dynamic Branch to Branch**
  (the Orchestrator configures it for all Edges by default, per the 7.0 Profile page) the first packets go that way, then, if a direct tunnel is possible, the initiating Edge builds one
  and tears it down after 180 seconds of traffic silence.

Because the overlay is independent of the underlay, we say the design has <Term id="transport-independence" />: the same policy and the
same tunnels work whichever kinds of circuit a site happens to have.

## What runs inside the tunnels

The tunnels use a protocol called <Term id="vcmp" /> and run on **UDP port 2426**. Arista's guide says VCMP is encrypted with
transport-mode IPsec, and it adds a small header to user packets (31 bytes) to support things like resequencing and error correction.
The mechanism that makes the tunnels clever is <Term id="dmpo" />:

- it **measures** loss, latency and jitter on every path, continuously, by tagging packets and, when no user traffic is flowing,
  sending probes every 100 ms;
- it **steers** traffic: a link that cannot deliver what an application needs (an <Term id="sla" /> failure, or an <Term id="outage" />)
  is detected within 300 to 500 ms and the flow is moved to a healthy link;
- it can **protect** traffic when steering is not enough, for example when there is only one link: correction techniques are switched
  on for as long as the link has problems.

A link that is up but degraded is in a <Term id="brownout" />; a link that has failed completely is in an outage. The guides use
those two words, so we do too. Later sections go deep on all of this. For now, remember the shape: **measure, steer, protect**.

## Policy-based control

What decides which link an application uses? A <Term id="business-policy" />, written once in the Orchestrator. Every application gets
**Auto** link steering by default, meaning DMPO picks the best links for that type of application. A rule can instead steer by
**Transport Group**, **Interface** or **WAN Link**. Policy applies to many Edges at once, which is how one change reaches 200 branches.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Two firewall facts worth memorizing">
  If an Edge will not come up behind a firewall, remember what it needs outbound: **TCP 443** to reach the Orchestrator, and
  **UDP 2426** (VCMP) for the tunnels. Arista states both for a virtual Edge. Inbound rules for hubs and NAT are covered later and are
  not fully confirmed in our sources yet.
</Callout>

## Three planes: a mental model

Network engineers often split any system into three jobs. We will use that split throughout the course.

- <Term id="management-plane" />: configure and monitor. In our picture, the Orchestrator.
- <Term id="control-plane" />: work out and share routes and policy. In our picture, route exchange between Edges and Gateways.
- <Term id="data-plane" />: carry user traffic. In our picture, packets moving through Edges and tunnels.

<Callout variant="gotcha" title="Arista's words, but not menu names">
  Arista's on-premises Orchestrator guide does use these words: the Orchestrator is the management plane, the Gateway (called a Controller there)
  is the control and routing plane, and the Hub cluster is the data plane. So the split is not something we invented, but you will not find a
  "control plane" menu in the product, and in the cloud-delivered service the Gateway also carries optional user traffic.
</Callout>

## Key takeaways

- Edge at the site, Gateway in the cloud, Orchestrator as the portal: three components, three jobs.
- The underlay is the circuits; the overlay is the tunnels built on top, over UDP 2426 (VCMP).
- DMPO measures, steers and protects; policy, defined centrally, tells it what each application needs.
- Management, control and data planes are wording from Arista's on-premises guide, not menu names in the Orchestrator.

## What would you look for in the VCO

- **Monitor > Edges** lists your Edges with their status, links and the **Gateways** they use, so the three components appear on one
  screen.
- Opening an Edge shows a **Links** tab (the WAN circuits and their measurements, including a transport-group view) and a **Paths**
  tab (the paths from this Edge to its SD-WAN peers, with quality scores). Roughly speaking, links are the underlay and paths are the overlay.
- **Configure > Profiles**, then the device settings, is where the Cloud VPN options (Branch to Hub, Branch to Branch, Dynamic Branch to
  Branch) are switched on, per segment.
- Business policy lives on an Edge's **Business Policy** tab (and for Profiles under **Configure > Profiles**).
