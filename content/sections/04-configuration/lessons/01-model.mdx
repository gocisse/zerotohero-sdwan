**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- name the layers an Edge's configuration comes from, general to specific;
- explain <Term id="inheritance" /> and the <Term id="override" /> checkbox in one sentence each;
- say where business policy and firewall rules fit in the same picture;
- ask "where is this setting coming from?" of any Edge behaviour.

## The question this section trains

You open an Orchestrator and an Edge is doing something. It sends one application over one link, drops some traffic, uses a VLAN. **Why does it behave this way, and where is that set?** Almost every answer is a layer.
We call the result the <Term id="effective-configuration" />: what the Edge actually ends up using. That phrase is our vocabulary, not a menu name.

## The layers

<Diagram id="cfg-layers" />

1. **Building blocks** (Enterprise level): <Term id="segment">segments</Term>, created under **Configure > Segments**, and network services. They belong to no Edge yet.
2. **Profile**: a template. Arista says Profiles provide a composite of the configurations created in Segments and Network Services, and that administrators use them to apply one template to many Edges at once.
3. **Edge**: each Edge is assigned a Profile at provisioning and inherits from it by default. On the Edge's **Device** tab, ticking **Override** next to an inherited setting lets you change it for that Edge only.
4. **Business policy and firewall rules** follow the same two-level pattern: rules exist at Profile level and the Edge has its own tab where an override can be made.

<Callout variant="info" title="Two levels, not three">
  Segments and network services are inputs to a Profile; they are not an extra layer that can override it. The two levels that compete are the **Profile** and the **Edge override**.
</Callout>

## A reusable method

When you see a behaviour, ask in this order:

1. Which kind of setting is it: interface or VLAN (Device tab), traffic treatment (business policy), or allow and drop (firewall)?
2. Does the **Edge** have an override for it?
3. If not, what does the **Profile** say?
4. Which **segment** does the traffic belong to?

<Callout variant="warning" title="What we could not confirm">
  How the Orchestrator marks an overridden setting on screen, and a complete list of which settings can be overridden, were not described in the pages we read. Look at the Override checkbox next to each setting in your own release.
</Callout>

## What you would see in the VCO

- **Configure > Profiles** lists Profiles with a **Used By** count of Edges; **Configure > Edges** lists Edges.
- An Edge's page has a **Device** tab for settings, and **Business Policy** and **Firewall** tabs for rules.
- **Configure > Segments** holds the segments a Profile can include.

## Key takeaways

- Layers: segments and network services, then Profile, then Edge override.
- Edges inherit from the Profile by default; Override changes one setting on one Edge.
- Business policy and firewall rules have the same Profile and Edge pattern.
- The reusable question: where is this setting coming from?

## What would you look for in the VCO

- Which Profile does this Edge use?
- Is the setting ticked Override on the Edge?
- Which segment is the traffic in?
